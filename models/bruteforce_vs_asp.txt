Agreement
  All AXps: identical AXp sets on 20/20 pairs (chess, heart disease).
  First AXp: the brute-force AXp is one of the AXps enumerated by ASP on 40/40
    pairs. The first AXp found depends on the search order (feature index order
    for the brute force, the MUS search in WASP), so sizes need not coincide.
  Brute force vs encoding.lp: every brute-force AXp is UNSAT and each of its
    one-smaller subsets SAT; with all AXps, every subset verdict agrees
    (5,760 subsets) and clingo enumerates exactly the same bin combinations
    with the same labels.

Brute force: benchmark_bruteforce.py [--first] --validate
  (models/bruteforce_performance.txt, models/bruteforce_first_performance.txt).
  Combinations are labelled by querying the sklearn forest as a black box under
  hard majority voting. All AXps: enumerate and label the whole bin space, then
  decide every subset; time = labelling + one pair. First AXp: deletion-based,
  one exhaustive sufficiency check per feature, each enumerating only the
  combinations agreeing with item 1 / item 2 on the subset and stopping at the
  first counterexample.
ASP: run_explanations.py --pairs 10 --repeats 3 (clingo 5.8.0 | wasp --mus,
  wasp commit 4332268); mean of 3 repetitions. The paper's Table 2 times,
  measured on the cluster (Intel Xeon 2.4 GHz), are 2-3x higher; AXp counts and
  sizes are the same.
Avg AXp size = mean over pairs of the per-pair mean size, as in Table 2.
